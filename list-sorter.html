🔄 List Sorter

Sort, reorder, shuffle, and organize lines of text. Choose from alphabetical, by length, random, reverse, or remove duplicates.

What Is a List Sorter?

A list sorter is a tool that rearranges lines of text in a specific order. It is commonly used to organize contact lists, shopping lists, task lists, data entries, and any collection of text items. Sorting alphabetically is the most common use, but list sorters also support sorting by length, randomization, reverse ordering, and duplicate removal — making them versatile for many tasks.

Sorting Options Explained

  • A-Z (Alphabetical): Sorts items from A to Z. Case-insensitive, so "apple" and "Banana" are sorted together.
  • Z-A (Reverse Alphabetical): Sorts items from Z to A. Useful for finding the last items in a list.
  • Shortest First: Sorts by line length, shortest to longest. Great for organizing words by size.
  • Longest First: Sorts by line length, longest to shortest. Useful for finding the longest entries.
  • Randomize: Shuffles the list into a random order. Perfect for picking random items or breaking patterns.
  • Reverse Order: Simply reverses the list without sorting. The last item becomes the first.
  • Remove Duplicates: Removes any duplicate lines, keeping only unique entries. Useful for cleaning data.

Common Use Cases

List sorters are useful in many everyday situations. Teachers use them to organize student names alphabetically. Programmers use them to sort data exports or clean up CSV files. Writers use them to organize brainstorming ideas or outline structures. Anyone working with lists of text data can benefit from quick and accurate sorting.
